title: Life & Society
headline: Europe, seen from one of its founding members
description: Essays on life in Luxembourg, European society and institutions, and how technology changes work.
standfirst:
  - Luxembourg is one of the six founding members of the European Communities and hosts several EU institutions. Close to half of its residents are foreign nationals, and a large share of its workforce crosses a border every morning.
  - That makes it a useful place to think about Europe. The questions of language, belonging, work and shared institutions show up here every day, at small scale.
focus:
  - title: Living in Luxembourg
    text: A small, multilingual country with a large international workforce. What it does well, what it struggles with, and what other European societies can learn from it.
  - title: Europe and its institutions
    text: How the Union makes decisions, why they take the time they take, and what citizens can reasonably expect from institutions they rarely see.
  - title: Languages and belonging
    text: Working in several languages changes how you think and who you can talk to. Notes on integration, identity and the everyday practice of living between cultures.
  - title: Technology, work and meaning
    text: Automation and AI are changing what professional work is. What stays human in that work, and how societies might share the gains and the costs.
questions:
  - What does European sovereignty mean for an ordinary citizen, beyond industrial policy?
  - How does a society stay cohesive when most of its daily life crosses borders and languages?
  - What should professional work look like once software writes a large share of software?
  - What can small states contribute to Europe that large states cannot?
