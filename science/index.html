--- layout: section section: science title: Science gn: true headline: Applied mathematics for data that is not normal description: Applied mathematics and statistics — heavy-tailed time series, the γ-order generalized normal distribution, information measures and stochastic optimisation. standfirst: - Many statistical models assume at some point that errors are normal. Financial returns, sensor noise and network delays often are not. I study models that let the data decide. - My doctoral research at Universidade Aberta brings the γ-order generalized normal distribution into autoregressive time-series models. The work is done in R and written up reproducibly. focus: - title: Heavy-tailed time series text: Autoregressive and state-space models whose errors have heavier or lighter tails than the normal, and what that changes for estimation, forecasting and risk. - title: Information measures text: Fisher's information, entropy, and the Kullback–Leibler and Hellinger distances as tools to compare fitted models and to measure how far data sit from normality. - title: Optimisation under uncertainty text: Stochastic programming, simheuristics and agile optimisation for decisions that must be made before the uncertainty resolves, such as routing in dynamic networks. - title: Statistical computing text: Likelihood estimation, Monte Carlo simulation and reproducible reporting in R, with the code published alongside the results. questions: - How should the shape parameter γ be estimated when observations are dependent rather than independent? - How far from normal does an error distribution have to be before it changes a forecast or a risk measure in a way that matters? - Which information measure is the most useful guide for choosing between competing error laws? - When does a fast heuristic lose less than the exact method costs in time? ---
Research

Adaptive γGN for two stochastic models

The autoregressive model and the signal process, with errors drawn from the γ-order generalized normal instead of the normal. Supervised by Prof. Christos P. Kitsos and Prof. Amílcar Oliveira, in the doctoral programme in Applied Mathematics & Modelling at Universidade Aberta.

  1. Classical AR modelsAR(1) and AR(p) with Gaussian errors: estimation, Fisher's information, diagnostics.
  2. The γGN familyProperties, maximum likelihood estimation, and distances to the normal.
  3. γ-order autoregressionAR(1) with γGN errors, with γ estimated from the data. A simulation study and financial series; γ = 2 is the reference.
  4. ExtensionsAR(p) and the signal process, once the AR(1) case is settled.
