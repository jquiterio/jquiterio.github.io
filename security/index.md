title: Security & Space
headline: Security for sovereign space infrastructure
description: Information security, certification and digital sovereignty for Europe's space and defence systems.
standfirst:
  - Satellites now carry government communications, navigation, earth observation and connectivity for places that fibre does not reach. That makes space systems part of Europe's critical infrastructure, and makes their security a matter of public interest.
  - I write about how security is designed into these systems, how it is assessed, and what sovereignty requires in practice. Everything here is based on public frameworks and my own views, not on any employer's systems.
focus:
  - title: Secure by design for space systems
    text: Space and ground segments have long lifetimes, constrained updates and many suppliers. Security has to be designed in from the start and carried through a lifecycle measured in decades.
  - title: Certification and accreditation
    text: Common Criteria (ISO/IEC 15408), the EU's EUCC scheme under the Cybersecurity Act, and the work of turning engineering evidence into an assurance case that an independent authority can accept.
  - title: Regulation in practice
    text: NIS2, the Cyber Resilience Act and the proposed EU Space Act are changing what operators and suppliers must demonstrate. What the rules ask for, and what it takes to meet them without drowning in paperwork.
  - title: Digital sovereignty and supply chains
    text: Sovereignty is less about ownership than about the ability to verify, operate and replace critical components. Software supply chains, cryptography and European alternatives.
questions:
  - How do you certify a system that changes every sprint?
  - Does sovereignty require ownership, control, or the ability to verify?
  - How should Europe's cybersecurity rules apply to assets in orbit?
  - How do you plan a post-quantum migration for a satellite designed to operate for fifteen years?
